# A packed-weight tolerance breach blocks dispatch until a dispatcher records the customer's answer

`0004-provisional-then-final-order-pricing.md` records what changed between the price a customer agreed to and the price of what was actually packed. This decision covers what *happens* when that change is large: when any per-weight line's `weight_delta_grams` exceeds its captured `tolerance_grams` in either direction, the Delivery cannot be picked up until a **dispatcher phones the customer** and records their answer. The customer may approve the revised Order, decline the affected lines and let the rest go out, or cancel outright. **No money moves** — this is a conversation and a lock, not a charge.

## Considered options

**A customer-facing in-app approval screen** was rejected. Map ticket 12 built the customer push pipeline with exactly two events and no retries or receipt-polling, on the reasoning that the dispatcher console is the reliability backstop. An approval that hangs off an unacknowledged push would strand packed, perishable goods with nobody accountable for chasing it. A phone call is also what the shop does today, and it matches the principle behind the tolerance band itself: **the burden is on the shop**. The shop changed the deal, so the shop chases.

**A new lifecycle status** — `AwaitingApproval` on the prep track, or its own courier status — was rejected in favour of a **guard on a transition**. `CONTEXT.md` deliberately keeps prep status and courier status independent, and this is the first rule that reads one to constrain the other. Expressing it as an approval outcome that blocks `Ready → Picked up` keeps both enums intact and confines the coupling to a single readable rule, rather than merging two state machines and owing new transitions in every direction.

**Whole-Order cancellation as the only refusal** was rejected. Losing a six-item Order because one ribeye came out 350 g heavy punishes the customer for the shop's imprecision. Lines may be declined individually — see partial continuation below.

**An auto-cancel timer** for an unanswered phone was rejected. Destroying a real Order full of already-cut meat on a timer is a destructive act with no human behind it; the goods are perishable, so physical reality already supplies the urgency, and map ticket 15's job-age attention badge makes the wait visible. A human decides when a customer is unreachable.

## Consequences

- **The gate is computed at one moment.** `Ready` already means "packed *and* priced" (ADR 0004), so every line's delta is knowable exactly then. The approval outcome is derived at that transition: `not_required` when every line is within its captured tolerance, `pending` when any line is not.
- **`abs(delta) > tolerance` — one rule, both directions.** A **shortfall** (packed under the estimate) gates dispatch exactly as an **excess** does. The rule is deliberately blind to direction, because a money-shaped rule would have read as "flag if it costs more" and let shortfalls out of the shop in silence. The dispatcher's *script* differs — a shortfall's first move is "can we cut more?", and the call happens only if it can't be fixed — but the Order does not move either way until the customer has answered.
- **The gate is enforced in software on every surface.** The dispatcher console blocks dispatch and states the reason; the driver app shows an assigned-but-held job with its "Picked up" action disabled. It is not enough to rely on the shop physically not handing over the bag: `Picked up` freezes the price record permanently, so one mistaken tap destroys the very thing the gate protects.
- **Orders became subtractive, but the record stays append-only.** A declined line is **soft-removed** (`removed_at`, `removed_by`, `removed_reason`) and excluded from every total; it is never deleted. Deleting it would erase the provisional-vs-final history ADR 0004 exists to preserve, and zeroing its totals would be indistinguishable from a free item.
- **Removal is reactive, never additive.** Only lines that breached tolerance may be declined. Nothing can be added, swapped, or increased. This is what keeps the flow from touching checkout: every submission-time gate — store hours (ticket 22), delivery radius (ticket 23), availability (ticket 26) — validated the Order once, at submission, and a subtractive-only change leaves all three still valid. A customer wanting to drop something that packed perfectly gets a cancel and a fresh Order.
- **Declining every line is a cancellation.** There is no Order with nothing on it. It is the same single action as an outright rejection, reached by a different route.
- **The delivery fee never moves.** ADR 0004 put it outside the delta machinery on purpose. A customer who declines most of their Order still owes the fee as captured at submission. Waiving it is a money decision in a system where no money moves — the shop's call to make verbally, off-system.
- **Approval binds to a price, so a later correction voids it.** ADR 0004 logs any weight retyped after the first entry. If a correction lands after an approval, the outcome is recomputed from scratch: still breaching, and it returns to `pending`; now inside the band, and it becomes `not_required`. An approved price can never be silently edited upward afterwards.
- **The record is a dispatcher's assertion, and that is enough.** It captures outcome, dispatcher, timestamp, a snapshot of the exact total approved (so drift is detectable), and an optional free-text note. No SMS confirmation or call recording — those are compliance artifacts for a payments track that does not exist. The note is cheap and carries what nothing else does: map ticket 10 keeps no location history and ticket 11's photo expires after 30 days, so it is often the only record of what was actually said.
- **Recomputation checks the human; it does not replace them.** When a dispatcher records approval of a revised Order, the surviving lines are re-checked. If one of them also breaches and was never discussed, the gate stays `pending` and names the missed line. Consent is something a human heard, not something arithmetic infers.
- **The removed meat gets no system state.** It returns to the case or is sold to someone else. Inventory is not modelled — availability is a manual toggle with no stock count (ticket 26).
- **The same principle has a pre-submission counterpart.** A customer never pays more than they agreed to without a human asking them first — this decision applies that at packing time, and the checkout gate stack applies it at submission: if a Product's catalog price rose while the item sat in the customer's cart, the submission is blocked and the higher price has to be acknowledged before the Order can be placed (a price *drop* applies silently). Two ends of the same Order's life, one rule. See map ticket [Cart & checkout assembly flow](https://github.com/vgviscayno/delivery-app/issues/29).
