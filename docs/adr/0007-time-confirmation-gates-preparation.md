# A requested delivery time is promised by a dispatcher on the phone, and blocks preparation until they answer

An Order may now be placed for a future delivery day, up to seven days ahead, and the customer may ask for a **30-minute window** within the Ordering window rather than taking ASAP. That request is only ever a request. **Nothing in the system promises a time.** On the delivery-day morning a dispatcher looks at the whole day, decides, and rings the customer to agree it — and the Order cannot advance to `Preparing` until they have. The promise exists because a person made it.

This reverses the reasoning in map ticket 25, which refused to show customers an ETA on the grounds that *a shown time reads as a promise*. The reversal is deliberate and narrow: ticket 25's ETA was machine-computed and continuously wrong, so the promise it implied was one nothing had agreed to. This is a human assertion, made once, with the day's full load in view. The distinction that matters is not whether a time is shown — it is who is accountable for it.

## Considered options

**A system-guaranteed slot** — the customer picks 14:30, the app confirms it at checkout — was rejected, and it was rejected on arithmetic rather than taste. Guaranteeing a slot means refusing the customer who would overfill it, which means knowing how many Orders two drivers can carry in half a day. That number does not exist anywhere: map ticket 34 deliberately ruled **no concurrency cap** on a driver, and van capacity sits in the map's fog as an operational number nobody has measured. A guarantee at checkout would have made measuring it a blocker and invented a capacity model to spend it on.

**A pure preference** — the customer states "afternoon", the dispatcher sequences around it, nothing is ever agreed — was rejected as too weak for what the feature is for. Someone ordering meat for tonight's dinner needs to know whether it will be there in time, and "we'll bear it in mind" does not tell them. The confirming call is what turns the feature from a hint into something a customer can plan around.

**A clock time with no tolerance** — "the driver knocks at 14:30" — was rejected as a promise the shop has no means to keep. There is no route optimisation (ADR 0001), no ETA anywhere (ticket 25), and, decisively, **no travel-time data at all**: ticket 41 found that no Mapbox Directions output may be stored at any tier with no paid unlock, so the road distance computed for ticket 23's radius check exists for a moment and is then gone. A dispatcher confirming an exact arrival minute is guessing. A 30-minute window is roughly the honest width of that guess.

**Confirming at submission** was rejected in favour of the **delivery-day morning**. On Friday a dispatcher cannot judge Tuesday's load, because most of Tuesday's Orders have not been placed yet; any answer then is a guess that later Orders invalidate. The morning prep block is when the day's Orders and their requested times are all visible together, it is an existing routine rather than a new one, and it still leaves most of a day to make the call.

**A `NeedsConfirmation` lifecycle status** was rejected in favour of a **guard on a transition**, for the same reason ADR 0005 rejected `AwaitingApproval`. The two are structurally identical — a dispatcher on the telephone, holding a transition open — and modelling one as a status and the other as a guard would have left the lifecycle owing an explanation for the difference. What the status was reaching for was **visibility**, and that is supplied without storing anything: the dispatcher gets a list section, the customer gets a derived label, both computed from facts already recorded. Ticket 35 established the precedent when it added a "Delayed" customer label without adding a status.

**A pre-dispatch status for an Order awaiting its delivery day** was rejected on the principle ticket 35 settled: a status needs a rule for *entering* it, and "the delivery day arrived" is a date comparison, not an event. Nothing happens to an advance Order at midnight. Both the console queue and the customer's Orders list filter on the delivery day instead.

## Consequences

- **Preparation is the thing being protected, and it is protected because it is irreversible.** `CONTEXT.md` holds that goods which must be packed again are a cancellation and a new Order. Packing at 06:30 an Order whose 14:30 request the shop cannot meet risks cut, weighed, priced meat for a customer who would rather have cancelled. The call comes first.
- **This is the second rule coupling the prep and courier tracks**, and they gate opposite ends of the journey: Time confirmation holds `Received → Preparing`, ADR 0005's Price approval holds `Ready → Picked up`. Both hold a transition open until a dispatcher has spoken to the customer. The tracks remain otherwise independent.
- **Three outcomes, not four.** `not_required` (the customer chose ASAP — the default, and what most Orders will carry forever), `pending`, `confirmed`. There is deliberately no "could not do it": an Order the shop and customer cannot agree a time for is **cancelled**, and Cancellation cause already records why. Adding a fourth outcome would have created a state with no transition out of it.
- **The requested time and the confirmed time are both stored.** A dispatcher who cannot do 14:30–15:00 offers an alternative, and a customer who accepts 15:30–16:00 leaves the outcome at `confirmed` — with the two times differing. This follows ADR 0005's shape, which records the exact total approved alongside what was originally agreed. An outcome value cannot express a renegotiation; two fields can.
- **An unreachable customer's Order is cancelled, not downgraded.** Holding indefinitely is not available to this gate the way it is to ADR 0005's: packed meat waits, but a delivery day expires, and 16:00 arrives with nothing packed. Downgrading to ASAP and delivering anyway was the first instinct and is **unsafe while the shop's payment model is unknown** — nothing in this system has ever recorded how the shop gets paid, and if it is cash at the door, a customer who asked for 14:30 and was sent something else at 09:00 can express their disagreement by refusing it, leaving the shop holding cut meat. Cancelling costs a sale; downgrading can cost stock. An Order still `pending` at the stated morning cut-off is cancelled with a Cancellation cause, which lands in "something else" with free text — the fixed list has no better fit, and widening it for one case was judged not worth it.
- **Submission stops being time-gated entirely.** Ticket 22 gated Order submission on whether the shop was open *now*. An advance Order makes that wrong: a customer ordering at 21:00 for Thursday is doing nothing unusual. The gate moves to the **choice of delivery time**, and `CONTEXT.md`'s **Ordering window** is redefined accordingly — it now constrains when an Order arrives, never when it may be placed. Ticket 34's last-call buffer becomes the **Same-day cutoff**, governing only whether *today* is still offerable.
- **Ticket 15's job-age badge must count from when an Order became due, not when it was submitted.** Otherwise a Tuesday Order placed on Friday shows as four days old on Tuesday morning and pulses red for the whole weekend before that.
- **Two new reasons for a Price approval call**, both specific to advance Orders. Between submission and packing a dispatcher may re-price a Product or withdraw its availability, and ADR 0003's snapshot means the Order still holds the old figures. Rather than build a second mechanism, the trigger for ADR 0005's existing call widens: from "a line breached its Weight tolerance" to "a recorded, objective fact about a line changed after the customer agreed to it". The invariant that mattered survives unharmed — a dispatcher's availability toggle and a rate edit are as auditable as a packed weight, so the shop still cannot drop a line it merely does not fancy packing. `CONTEXT.md`'s **Removed line item** widens from "breaching lines" to "affected lines" to match.
- **That call is triggered by the edit, not by packing.** A dispatcher who re-prices a Product sees every affected advance Order immediately, in ticket 31's `Awaiting price call` section. Discovering it at 06:00 on delivery morning would work, but knowing days earlier is strictly better and costs nothing.
- **No push notification and no timer accompany that call.** Both were considered and rejected, consistent with ADR 0005: ticket 12's pipeline is fire-and-forget with no retries, consent to a revised price may only be taken on the Account phone, and a customer who ordered on Friday for Tuesday will not answer within minutes. A notification could offer no action — the customer cannot add a line or approve a total in the app — so it would deliver anxiety and nothing else, while a synchronised timer would turn one rate edit into a queue of simultaneous calls for a single dispatcher.
