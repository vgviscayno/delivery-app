Delivery app · questions for you

How your drivers work

Four things we can't decide without you. They're about how the shop actually runs, not about technology — so there are no wrong answers here, only the real ones.

How to answer

Rough answers are fine. “It depends — for example…” is more useful to us than a firm rule. Where we've suggested something, it's only a starting point; disagreeing with it is genuinely helpful.

Question 1 shapes the other three, so if you only have a minute, answer that one first.

1

Your drivers and their hours

  • How many drivers do you have?
  • Do they work set hours — say 8am to 5pm — or do they come in when there's work to do?
  • At the end of the day, does the company phone go home with the driver, or does it stay at the shop?

Why we're asking Almost everything below changes depending on whether “the working day” is a fixed thing or a loose one.

2

Should a driver tell the app when they've started work?

A

The driver clocks in. They tap “Start work” when they begin for the day, and “Finish work” when they're done.

B

The app works it out by itself. If the driver's phone is on and they aren't already out with orders, the app treats them as ready for a job.

Why we're asking Option B is one less thing for a driver to remember — but the app can't tell the difference between “on lunch”, “at the dentist” and “ready for the next job”, so your office may send someone a delivery when they aren't there to take it. Option A costs one tap at each end of the day, and in exchange your office gets an honest picture of who's around.

Our suggestion

A — the clock-in.

3

When a driver isn't carrying an order, should the office see where they are?

As things stand, a driver only appears on the office map while they're actually out delivering something. Between jobs, they disappear from it.

The catch: when a new order comes in, your office wants to send it to whoever is nearest — and it can't work out who's nearest among drivers it can't see.

A

Visible for the whole working day. From “Start work” to “Finish work”, and never outside those hours.

B

Visible only during an actual delivery. Your office picks a driver by name and by memory, rather than by who's closest.

Worth knowing, either way The app keeps no record of anywhere a driver has been. Nothing is saved and there's no history to look back through — only where somebody is right now, and only while it matters. It can't be used to review someone's day afterwards.
Our suggestion

A — but this one is yours as the employer rather than ours, and it's the kind of thing your drivers should hear directly from you.

4

How many orders can one driver carry at once?

A driver can be given several deliveries at a time and decide for themselves which to drop off next. Is there a number that's simply too many — because the van is full, or because it gets confusing?

A

A hard limit. The app refuses to give a driver a sixth order — or whatever the right number turns out to be.

B

No limit. The app shows your office how many each driver is already carrying, and the person assigning the work decides.

Why we're asking Any limit we set now is a guess. Guess low and it blocks your office on your busiest day; guess high and it does nothing at all.

Our suggestion

B for now — then a real number later, once you've seen how much actually fits in a van.

Thank you — these four answers unlock the whole driver side of the app.
