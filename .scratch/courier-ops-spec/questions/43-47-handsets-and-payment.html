Delivery app · questions for you

Phones, and getting paid

Four more, following on from the last set. The first is a small clarification. The other three are about money — not about the app handling any, which it won't, but about how you already get paid.

How to answer

Same as before — rough answers are fine, and “it depends” is a real answer. Questions 2 to 4 describe things your shop already does, so there's nothing to decide; we just need to know how it works today.

Question 2 is the one that unblocks the most, so if you only have a minute, answer that one.

1

Whose phone is it?

Last time you told us the phone “stays at the shop and vehicle.” We want to be sure we've read that the right way, because it can mean two different things.

A

Each driver has their own phone. It lives in their van during the day and comes back to the shop at night — but nobody else uses it.

B

Each van has a phone. It lives in the van, and whoever is driving that van today uses it. Tomorrow it might be somebody else.

Why we're asking It changes how a driver starts their day. Under A, the phone knows who the driver is permanently — they never sign in, they just open the app and tap “Start work”. Under B, the phone can't know who's holding it, so the driver signs in as themselves each morning — and then the separate “Start work” tap is redundant. Signing in is starting work. One step instead of two, but a step they can't skip.

There's a second reason it matters Your office sees each driver's position on a map while they're out delivering. Under A that name is always right. Under B it's right only if whoever took the van that morning remembered to sign in as themselves — otherwise the map shows yesterday's driver.

With two drivers and two vans these probably amount to the same thing today. They come apart the first time a driver takes the other van. If the honest answer is “it's A now, but I wouldn't want to promise that”, tell us — that's useful too.

2

How does a customer pay you today?

Tick whatever actually happens — and if it's a mix, say roughly how often each one comes up.

A

Cash, handed to the driver at the door.

B

Bank transfer or GCash, before the driver sets off.

C

Bank transfer or GCash, after delivery — same day, next day, whenever they get to it.

D

An account. Regular customers billed weekly or monthly, settled in one go.

E

Something else — tell us what.

Why we're asking The answer decides what we do with an order that has already been cut and packed when something goes wrong. If the customer has already paid, delivering it anyway is safe — worst case they're mildly annoyed. If they pay cash at the door, the same delivery is a real risk: they can simply refuse the bag, and you're holding meat you cut for them that nobody else ordered.

This has already cost us once We hit exactly this situation and had to take the cautious route — cancelling an order rather than delivering it — purely because we didn't know the answer. That was the right call that time. It won't always be available.
3

Does the driver handle money?

A

Yes — they take cash, and settle up with the shop at the end of the day.

B

No — money never touches the driver.

Why we're asking If your driver is carrying cash, the phone they're holding is also, in effect, a till. That raises the stakes on a few things we've designed loosely — who's signed into the phone, what gets recorded when goods come back undelivered, and what proof you want taken at the door.

4

What happens now when a customer won't take the delivery?

The driver is at the door and the customer says no — wrong day, changed their mind, not what they expected, nobody told them the price went up.

  • Does this happen? Once a month, twice a year, never?
  • What does the driver do — ring the shop, bring it back, leave it anyway?
  • Does the customer still owe you for it?

Why we're asking The app currently has no answer for a driver standing at a door with a bag the customer won't take. There's no button for it, because nobody has told us what the right button would do. Whatever you already do is almost certainly the right thing to build.

One thing worth saying plainly

None of this means the app starts handling payments. It won't take card details, it won't charge anyone, and it won't move money. We're asking because the app has to decide when it's safe to deliver an order and when it isn't — and that turns entirely on whether you've already been paid.

Thank you — question 2 in particular unblocks decisions we've been working around.
